Malaysia Crime Patterns across All States

Malaysia Crime Levels

Since 2016, urban states such as Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya and Selangor consistently record higher crime levels compared to rural states. These states serve as major economic and transportation hubs, attracting large populations and increased social activity, which tend to correlate with higher crime incidence. Throughout the years, the trend is encouraging as the intensity of crime noticeably declines across nearly all states, including previously high-crime states like Putrajaya and Selangor, which strongly indicates nationwide enforcement efforts and community policing initiatives have been effective in driving down crime over time.

Assault vs Property Crimes

Since 2016, property crimes overwhelmingly dominate total reported cases every year, consistently making up the largest share of offences nationwide. Prior to major crime reform initiatives, a notable downward trend in overall crime after 2016, especially a sharp drop in 2020 coincides with COVID-19 movement restrictions, which limited public interaction and mobility, leading a temporary but pronounced fall in both property and assault crimes.

Property crime clearly dominates, suggesting that most crimes are non-violent and financially motivated. Assault crimes exist but make up a smaller fraction indicating they are less frequent compared to theft. Murder and rape are the smallest slices, highlighting that while serious, they occur relatively rare compared to theft-based crimes.

Socioeconomic Factors & Crime Patterns

The relationship between crime rates and poverty levels shows interesting patterns from 2016-2023. While poverty rates have steadily declined from 5.7% to an estimated 2.5%, crime rates peaked in 2016 and then significantly decreased, particularly during the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic. This suggests that effective poverty reduction policies and targeted crime prevention strategies have contributed to safer communities nationwide. The forecasted poverty trend (dashed line) indicates continued improvement.

The scatter plot reveals no strong linear correlation between unemployment and crime rates across Malaysian states and years. Federal territories cluster in high crime, moderate unemployment regions, while rural states show lower crime levels with varying unemployment. Notably, urban states like Selangor maintain moderate crime despite economic opportunities. This indicates that urban density and economic activity may be stronger predictors of crime than unemployment alone, highlighting the complex nature of socioeconomic crime factors.
